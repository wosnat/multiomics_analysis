Provisional reading

MED4 raises its capacity to take up and process ammonium (amt1), urea (urt with urease) and cyanate (cyn with cyanase) as part of the N-starvation response. It sustains that capacity through 89 days of coculture; in the dying axenic culture these import systems fall back while the N-stress regulators stay up. Expression cannot name the compound used. In calibration experiments urt, cyn and the nitrite system focA rise on substrates other than their own, so their expression does not report what is present; amt1 was not calibrated. The dipeptide system (dpp) is raised in coculture protein but unresolved. No other nitrogen route shows a coculture signal beyond starvation. The nitrate channel clc2 is irrelevant (MED4 can’t use nitrate), and the broadly annotated routes were explored only. Envelope genes (murJ, and more weakly tolC, acrA, bamA) are up in coculture and not in the dying axenic culture. Because the axenic comparison is with a declining culture, none of these contrasts separates a partner effect from survival. “No signal” is not evidence that no other compound is taken up.

1. The import systems report nitrogen stress, not the compound

What the bars compare (Tolonen et al. 2006, microarrays). Each bar is the gene’s rank in one experiment, compared against cells in normal N-replete Pro99 medium.

  • Growth on a sole N source (cyanate or urea for MED4; nitrite or urea for MIT9313): cells adapted to that compound as their only nitrogen, sampled in steady exponential growth. There is a single timepoint (“steady state”).
  • N deprivation, 24 h: cells 24 h after transfer to nitrogen-free medium (green bars). The full 0–48 h series is in the heatmaps below. MED4’s response peaks at 6–12 h; MIT9313’s keeps growing to 48 h.

Short names: amt1 = ammonium transporter; urt = urea transporter; cyn = cyanate transporter; focA = nitrite transporter (with nirA, nitrite reductase); dpp = di/oligopeptide transporter; urease = urea → ammonium; cynS = cyanase (cyanate → ammonium); glsF = glutamate synthase; markers = glnA (glutamine synthetase), ntcA (N-control regulator), glnB (PII), pipX. Non-N reference transporters: pst = phosphate, phn = phosphonate, sul = sulfate, fut/idiA = iron, mnt = manganese, sbtA = bicarbonate.

In Tolonen’s experiments, MED4 was grown on cyanate or urea as its only nitrogen source. On cyanate, the starvation marker glnA is up and so are both the cyanate (cyn) and urea (urt) systems. On urea, neither system rises and ntcA is near the bottom. Growing on cyanate is itself nitrogen-stressful; growing on urea is not. The systems follow the cell’s nitrogen status, so their expression can’t name which compound is present.

The same holds in a second strain. Tolonen also grew MIT9313 on nitrite or urea as its only nitrogen source. Both the nitrite system (focA + nirA) and urt are at the very top on both substrates: focA 1.000 / 0.999, urt 0.98 / 0.99. ntcA is high on both. The urease is higher on nitrite (0.95–0.98) than on urea (0.66–0.83). Each system passes the test on its own substrate and fails the specificity test.

[interpretation] In two strains on three substrates, the nitrogen import systems rise together whenever the nitrogen source isn’t ammonium. They report the cell’s nitrogen status, not the compound. (MED4 on urea is the exception, where nothing rises.)

Pre-registered consequenceThe calibration checks C2 (urt on urea) and C3 (each system is specific to its substrate) fail. As set in the proposal, the consumer side reports elevated import capacity only and names no compound. The proposal also said that urt and cyn rising together on one substrate is evidence of shared NtcA control (its reading (i)).

2. In late coculture, protein shows full nitrogen-starvation induction, with amt1 joining late

  • amt1’s rank minus the median of the urt and cyn subunits goes from −0.27 at day 18 to about 0 by day 60. amt1 catches up with the other systems; they don’t fall back. If ammonium supply partly relieved N stress (reading (ii)), urea and cyanate should have been damped; no damping is detectable, but ranks are at their ceiling here, so this is a weak test.
  • [interpretation] amt1’s late rise could be slower protein build-up, a later NtcA threshold, or ammonium-dependent regulation. Expression can’t separate these.

4. Why protein and RNA disagree: the coculture already looked N-stressed during exponential growth

Culture states (researcher’s account). Axenic day 11: exponential, beginning to run out of nitrogen. Axenic “day 14”: sampled around day 14 and paired with coculture day 18; already late decline, most cells dying, very starved. Axenic cultures move through N exhaustion and decline faster than cocultures, so the same calendar day is not the same stage. The coculture’s state at these points is not known.

  • Day 11. Both cultures are in exponential growth. The axenic culture is starting to run out of N, yet coculture N-regulon RNA is still higher. The coculture was at least as N-stressed as an axenic culture nearing exhaustion.
  • The anchor RNA compares each culture with its own exponential phase. The coculture’s exponential phase already had high N-regulon RNA, so its later days look “down” while the proteins are up. Coculture RNA can’t be read as “not induced”.
  • Day 18 vs the dying axenic culture. The coculture has lower N-regulon RNA and higher ribosomal and P/S/Fe transporter RNA. [interpretation] All this says is that the coculture is less starved than a dying axenic culture. It can’t separate partial N relief from “not dying”, and the coculture’s own state is unknown.
Affects the planThe proposal used axenic day 14 as the early-starvation reference (the “stands out beyond early axenic starvation” margin, and the “also up in early axenic starvation” flag). That sample is a deep-starvation, declining culture, so the label’s wording and the meaning of its margin need revisiting when we return to classification. The proposal’s “time-matched” read (coculture d18 vs axenic d14) is matched by day, not by physiological stage.

5. Other observations

  • The N-replete controls show little. In the MED4 control, the N genes sit at the table’s overall level (0.47 vs non-N 0.46), so their “down” calls reflect the table’s skew, not something N-specific. MIT9313 natF is a single subunit in one of two cultures, and a phosphonate gene is up in the same table.

6. Published co-expression clusters give the same picture

Tolonen’s study clustered MED4 genes by how they respond to N deprivation: 9 groups, 410 genes. Two groups rise strongly within 3–6 h: c1 (cynA, glnA, urtA) and c2 (ntcA, glnB, cynB + others). Together they form the N-starvation stimulon, the genes that respond together to this stimulus. That is an empirical gene set to use instead of our three hand-picked markers. An iron-starvation study (Thompson 2011) offers the same for iron.

  • Same conclusion as with the markers. In protein, the coculture’s N-starvation response stays at the level of a late-decline axenic culture through day 89, with no damping detectable at the rank ceiling (marker fold changes are mixed late: ntcA eases, glnB rises).
  • dpp isn’t in any cluster, nor are cynS, urtC/D/E or the broad systems, so the clusters can’t settle the dpp question.
  • [interpretation] A first hint of iron stress late in coculture: the iron-stress cluster is modestly raised in coculture protein (its RNA is not). It rests on 20 of 59 genes detected in protein and shares cynA with the N set, so it needs checking before it carries weight.

Open questions for classification, when we return to it

  • Labels now use protein only (agreed). Should RNA be read against the day-11 coculture-vs-axenic baseline rather than each culture’s own exponential phase?
  • Should multi-locus systems like dpp be judged per locus? Its dppB/dppA locus responds to starvation while dppC/ddpD don’t follow.
  • Should the label’s comparison with axenic day 14 count amt1’s late rise (+0.31 margin) as standing out, given that cyn was already at its maximum by day 14 (+0.01 margin)?
